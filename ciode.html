What is this project, really?

No jargon. No assumptions. Just the idea, explained slowly, with real numbers and real examples — the way you'd explain it to a smart friend who has zero background in loans, apps, or AI.

Part 1
The problem, in one story

Meet Ramesh. He lives in a village. He has saved ₹1,00,000. He wants to open a small shop. That's it — that's the whole starting point.

Ramesh has two questions, and right now, nobody can answer either one properly:

Question 1

"Will people actually buy from my shop?" Is there already a shop just like his nearby? Are there enough people around to buy from him? What should he charge?

Question 2

"If I borrow money from the bank, how much can I get, and can I actually pay it back every month?" He has ₹1,00,000 saved — but he has no idea what that turns into once a bank loan is added.

Today, Ramesh just guesses. He copies what his cousin did in a different village. Sometimes that works out. Often it doesn't — and then he can't pay his loan back, and he's in worse trouble than before he started.

The project's whole job is to answer those two questions properly — using real information about his actual location and his actual savings, instead of guesswork.

Part 2
The two halves of the app

Think of the app as having exactly two jobs. Everything else is just details underneath these two jobs.

Half 1 — "Will this business work here?"

This half looks at Ramesh's actual village and checks things like:

  • How many people live close enough to realistically buy from him
  • Whether there's already a similar shop nearby, and how many
  • What price is fair to charge — not too high, not too low
  • What could go wrong — for example, maybe sales drop badly during a certain season

Half 2 — "Can Ramesh actually afford this loan?"

This half does the money math. Let's actually do it together, with real numbers.

Worked example

Ramesh has ₹1,00,000 of his own money. Under the government rule this project is built around, your own money always has to be exactly 10% of the total project. So:

Total project size = ₹1,00,000 ÷ 10% = ₹10,00,000

Loan amount = 90% of that = ₹9,00,000

Because the project is bigger than ₹1.4 lakh, it falls into a specific loan category with an 8% yearly interest rate, 7 years to repay, and — importantly — no payment required for the first 6 months, to give the business time to get started. After doing the math, Ramesh would pay about ₹15,720 every month once payments begin.

That's the core trick of the whole app: you tell it your savings, and it tells you the size of business you can realistically build, and exactly what you'll owe every month. No guessing.

Part 3
Why not just ask a normal AI chatbot this?

Good question — here's the honest answer.

The risk

A normal AI chatbot can sound very confident even when it's wrong. If you ask it "are there many shops like mine near me?", it might make up a plausible-sounding answer without actually knowing anything about your specific village. That's fine for casual chat — but it's dangerous when the answer affects a real loan someone is about to take.

So this project is built with two safety rules baked in:

RuleWhat it means in practice
The money math is never "guessed"All loan, interest, and monthly-payment numbers come from a strict calculator — not from the AI's imagination. The AI is only allowed to explain those numbers in plain language.
Every fact gets an honesty labelLocal facts (like "how many shops are nearby") are shown with a tag: High confidence (we're pretty sure), Medium confidence (educated estimate), or Not verified (please double-check yourself). Nothing is shown as certain unless it actually is.

Part 5
What does Ramesh actually type into the app?

Very little. That's on purpose — most rural users don't want to type long forms on a phone. So the flow is mostly tapping:

  1. Pick a language — Telugu, Hindi, or English
  2. Pick a location — from a list, not by typing a village name
  3. Enter savings amount — using a big number pad, with quick "₹50,000 / ₹1,00,000" shortcut buttons
  4. Pick a business type — shown as picture cards (dairy, shop, tailoring, repair, etc.)
  5. (Optional) Add extra details — like training, land, or existing equipment

That's the whole input. In under three minutes, Ramesh has told the app everything it needs.

Part 6
What does Ramesh get back?

A single report, in his own language, containing:

1. Your loan plan — how much you can borrow, your monthly payment, and when payments actually start.

2. Who might buy from you — a rough count of people nearby who could realistically become customers.

3. Who you're competing with — nearby similar businesses, honestly labeled if the data isn't fully certain.

4. A fair price to charge — based on local costs and what people nearby can typically afford.

5. What could go wrong — risks like a bad season, or too much local competition.

6. Where every fact came from — so nothing is a black box.

7. A button to talk to a real human — because this app is meant to prepare Ramesh before he visits the bank, not replace the bank officer.

Part 7
How is it actually built? (in plain terms)

You can think of the whole system as four simple pieces working together:

PiecePlain explanation
The screen (what Ramesh taps)Simple, big buttons, works even on a slow phone and weak internet.
The calculator (does the loan math)A strict, rule-based program — it never estimates or guesses the numbers, it just calculates them exactly the same way every time.
The researcher (gathers local facts)Looks up whatever real data exists about the area — nearby shops, population — and honestly flags when that data is thin or missing.
The explainer (the AI language part)Takes the calculator's numbers and the researcher's facts, and turns them into a friendly, plain-language report in Ramesh's language. It is not allowed to invent new numbers of its own.

And importantly: for the hackathon version, all of this is built using free tools — because there's no budget yet, and the goal is to prove the idea works before spending any money on it.

Part 8
What could go wrong, and how the app protects against it

If this happens......the app does this
The AI tries to state something as fact that isn't actually verifiedA built-in checker catches it and blocks it before it reaches Ramesh
There's no data for Ramesh's tiny villageIt uses the nearest larger area's data instead, and clearly says so
Someone tries to trick the AI by typing sneaky instructions into a text boxThe app treats all typed text as plain information, never as a command it has to obey
The loan looks financially risky for that personA warning is shown before they apply — not after they're already in trouble
The internet or an outside service fails during a live demoThe core loan-math calculator still works completely offline as a backup

Part 9
The one idea to remember

If you forget everything else, remember this This is a phone app that does two honest jobs for a rural entrepreneur: it checks whether a business idea will actually work in their specific village, and it does careful, accurate math about how much they can borrow and what they'll owe — and it always tells them clearly when it's certain versus when it's just estimating. It helps someone walk into a bank prepared. It never pretends to be the bank itself.
